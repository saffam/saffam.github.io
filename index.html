Projects

Project 1: Global Health Expenditure Efficiency

Status: Complete | Tools: Python, pandas, matplotlib | Data: WHO Global Health Expenditure Database (2015–2022)

Question: Which countries achieve different patterns of health financing in terms of government spending, out-of-pocket expenditure, and health spending relative to GDP?

Key Findings:

  • Large spending gap: Health expenditure per capita varies substantially across countries, with the United States spending considerably more than lower-income countries included in the analysis.
  • Government financing matters: Countries differ substantially in the share of healthcare spending financed through government sources versus households.
  • US spending is an outlier: The United States has a substantially higher health expenditure share of GDP than the European countries examined.
  • Out-of-pocket burden: Several lower-income countries rely much more heavily on direct household payments for healthcare.

Policy Implications:

  • The analysis highlights substantial differences in healthcare financing structures across countries.
  • High household out-of-pocket spending can create financial barriers to healthcare access.
  • Cross-country comparisons provide a useful starting point for examining health-system financing and efficiency.

Project 2: The COVID-19 Health Financing Shock

Status: Complete | Tools: Python, pandas, matplotlib | Data: WHO Global Health Expenditure Database (2019–2021)

Question: When COVID-19 hit, how did government and household healthcare financing change across different health systems?

Key Findings:

  • Different financing responses: Countries varied considerably in how public and household health expenditure changed during the pandemic.
  • Household burden: Some countries experienced increases in out-of-pocket spending despite increases in overall health expenditure.
  • Cross-country variation: The analysis identified different patterns of financing response across the countries examined.
  • Health-system resilience: Changes in public financing provide one perspective for examining how health systems responded to the financial shock of COVID-19.

Policy Implications:

  • Government financing capacity can influence how health systems absorb major shocks.
  • Changes in household out-of-pocket spending are important when assessing the financial consequences of a health crisis.
  • Public financing data can provide an initial basis for comparing health-system responses to major disruptions.

Project 4: Semaglutide Budget Impact Models

Status: Complete | Tools: Python, pandas, data analysis and visualisation | Data: Public healthcare and official population sources

Question: What could be the potential five-year budget impact of semaglutide-based obesity treatment across different healthcare systems?

This project develops comparative Budget Impact Models (BIMs) for semaglutide (Wegovy) obesity management across the United Kingdom, United States, and Italy.

Model components:

  • Adult population estimation
  • Obesity prevalence and eligible population estimation
  • Treatment uptake scenarios
  • Annual treatment costs
  • Healthcare cost offsets where country-specific evidence is available
  • Five-year gross and net budget impact
  • Price and assumption sensitivity analysis

Research approach: The models use publicly available population, epidemiological, healthcare, and pharmaceutical data. Country-specific assumptions and sources are documented in the repository to make the calculations traceable and reproducible.

Project 5: European Food Policy & Obesity Analysis

Status: Complete | Focus: Health Economics, Public Health, Food Policy | Tools: Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas and data visualisation

Question: How do obesity prevalence and selected food-policy environments differ across European countries, and what patterns can be observed between policy measures and population obesity outcomes?

This project examines publicly available health and food-policy data to explore differences in obesity outcomes across European countries with different policy environments.

Analysis includes:

  • Collection and preparation of publicly available country-level data
  • Data cleaning and harmonisation
  • Cross-country comparison of obesity outcomes
  • Comparison of selected food-policy environments
  • Exploratory statistical analysis
  • Data visualisation using Python
  • Interpretation of findings within the limitations of observational cross-country analysis

Research approach: The project is exploratory rather than causal. Differences between countries may reflect many factors beyond food policy, including demographic, socioeconomic, dietary, healthcare, and measurement differences.

Why this project matters: Food policy is an important area for public health and health economics research. Comparing policy environments and population outcomes provides a starting point for identifying questions that could be investigated further using longitudinal or more detailed country-level data.
